# KPI Catalogue

> **Synthetic data.** All numbers in this project come from a generated dataset, not a real company.
> This file is generated by `src/kpi_catalogue.py`. Edit the definitions there and re-run `python -m src.kpi_catalogue`.

Every KPI uses the **cleaned** order lines (one row = one order line). A ratio is left empty, never 0 and never an error, when its denominator is 0. SQL, Python and Power BI (DAX) must give the same value for the same filters.

## Overview

| KPI | Formula | Main stakeholders |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | SUM(revenue) over order lines, where line revenue = quantity x unit_price | Business manager, Sales manager, Product/category manager, Regional manager, Business analyst |
| Cost | SUM(cost) over order lines (line cost is already the total for the line) | Business manager, Product/category manager, Business analyst |
| Profit | SUM(profit), where line profit = revenue - cost (so profit = Revenue - Cost) | Business manager, Sales manager, Product/category manager, Regional manager, Business analyst |
| Profit margin % | Profit / Revenue x 100. Not calculated (empty) when Revenue is 0 | Business manager, Product/category manager, Regional manager, Business analyst |
| Distinct orders | COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) | Business manager, Sales manager, Regional manager, Business analyst |
| Order lines | COUNT(*) over order lines | Business analyst, Sales manager |
| Units sold | SUM(quantity) | Product/category manager, Regional manager |
| Average order value (AOV) | Revenue / Distinct orders. Not calculated when there are no orders | Business manager, Sales manager, Regional manager |
| Active customers | COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) | Sales manager, Business manager |
| Revenue per customer | Revenue / Active customers. Not calculated when there are no customers | Sales manager, Business manager |
| Repeat-customer rate | Customers with 2 or more distinct orders / Active customers (a fraction from 0 to 1) | Sales manager, Business analyst |

| Top-N customer share | Revenue of the N biggest customers / Total revenue x 100 | Sales manager, Business manager |

## Details

### Revenue

- **Business meaning:** Total value of goods sold. The basic measure of business size.
- **Formula:** SUM(revenue) over order lines, where line revenue = quantity x unit_price
- **Data source:** sales_lines (clean order lines)
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice: total, month, week, region, category, segment, product, customer
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business manager, Sales manager, Product/category manager, Regional manager, Business analyst
- **Interpretation caveats:** Gross sales in one generic currency. No discounts, returns or taxes are modelled.

### Cost

- **Business meaning:** Total cost of the goods sold.
- **Formula:** SUM(cost) over order lines (line cost is already the total for the line)
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business manager, Product/category manager, Business analyst
- **Interpretation caveats:** Product cost only. Overheads, shipping and staff costs are not in the data.

### Profit

- **Business meaning:** What is left after the cost of goods. Shows which activity actually earns money.
- **Formula:** SUM(profit), where line profit = revenue - cost (so profit = Revenue - Cost)
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business manager, Sales manager, Product/category manager, Regional manager, Business analyst
- **Interpretation caveats:** Gross profit, not net profit. A negative value means the slice sold below cost.

### Profit margin %

- **Business meaning:** How many cents of profit each unit of revenue produces. Compares slices of different size fairly.
- **Formula:** Profit / Revenue x 100. Not calculated (empty) when Revenue is 0
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business manager, Product/category manager, Regional manager, Business analyst
- **Interpretation caveats:** Never average margins of slices. Recalculate it from summed profit and summed revenue, otherwise small slices get too much weight.

### Distinct orders

- **Business meaning:** Number of separate purchases (orders), regardless of how many products each contained.
- **Formula:** COUNT(DISTINCT order_id)
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business manager, Sales manager, Regional manager, Business analyst
- **Interpretation caveats:** Not additive across categories or products: one order can contain several. It is additive across regions and segments because an order has exactly one of each.

### Order lines

- **Business meaning:** Number of product lines sold (rows in the data). Used to see basket size, not to count orders.
- **Formula:** COUNT(*) over order lines
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business analyst, Sales manager
- **Interpretation caveats:** Never use this as the order count. It is larger than the number of orders.

### Units sold

- **Business meaning:** Total quantity of items sold.
- **Formula:** SUM(quantity)
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Product/category manager, Regional manager
- **Interpretation caveats:** Mixes cheap and expensive items, so units can rise while revenue falls.

### Average order value (AOV)

- **Business meaning:** Typical size of one purchase.
- **Formula:** Revenue / Distinct orders. Not calculated when there are no orders
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business manager, Sales manager, Regional manager
- **Interpretation caveats:** Divides by orders, not by order lines. Heavily influenced by a few very large orders (Corporate customers), so compare it by segment.

### Active customers

- **Business meaning:** How many different customers bought in the selected period.
- **Formula:** COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id)
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Sales manager, Business manager
- **Interpretation caveats:** Depends on the period length. The count for a year is not the sum of the monthly counts.

### Revenue per customer

- **Business meaning:** Average revenue earned from each active customer.
- **Formula:** Revenue / Active customers. Not calculated when there are no customers
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice
- **Intended stakeholders:** Sales manager, Business manager
- **Interpretation caveats:** An average. A few large customers pull it up, so read it together with customer concentration.

### Repeat-customer rate

- **Business meaning:** Share of active customers who bought on more than one occasion in the period.
- **Formula:** Customers with 2 or more distinct orders / Active customers (a fraction from 0 to 1)
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/kpi_summary.sql`, `src.metrics.compute_kpis`
- **Aggregation level:** Any slice (customer_metrics.sql gives the per-customer flag)
- **Intended stakeholders:** Sales manager, Business analyst
- **Interpretation caveats:** A repeat-purchase indicator only. It is NOT a churn or 'at risk' measure, which would need a defensible definition. In the default synthetic dataset customers order very often over two years, so the rate is close to 100% and tells little.

### Revenue / profit contribution %

- **Business meaning:** Share of the total that one product, category, region, segment or customer provides.
- **Formula:** Revenue of the item / Total revenue of the same filtered selection x 100 (same for profit)
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/by_product.sql`, `src.metrics.breakdown`
- **Aggregation level:** Product, category, region, customer segment (by_region, by_category, by_segment, by_product)
- **Intended stakeholders:** Product/category manager, Regional manager, Business manager
- **Interpretation caveats:** Shares always refer to the current filter. Change a slicer and every share changes.

### Top-N customer share

- **Business meaning:** How concentrated revenue is: the share earned by the largest N customers (or top X% of customers).
- **Formula:** Revenue of the N biggest customers / Total revenue x 100
- **Data source:** Per-customer revenue (customer_metrics)
- **Implemented in:** `sql/customer_metrics.sql`, `src.metrics.top_n_customer_share`
- **Aggregation level:** Whole selection (computed from the customer table)
- **Intended stakeholders:** Sales manager, Business manager
- **Interpretation caveats:** Describes concentration, not risk. Whether a high share is a problem depends on the business.

### Period-over-period change

- **Business meaning:** How much a KPI moved compared with the previous period of the same length.
- **Formula:** Absolute change = current - previous. % change = (current - previous) / ABS(previous) x 100. Not calculated when previous is 0 or missing
- **Data source:** sales_lines
- **Implemented in:** `sql/period_comparison.sql`, `src.metrics.compare_periods`
- **Aggregation level:** Any two non-overlapping periods; monthly_trend.sql gives month-over-month
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business manager, Sales manager, Product/category manager, Regional manager, Business analyst
- **Interpretation caveats:** Ignores seasonality: December is expected to beat November. A short previous period (for example one quiet week) can make the percentage look dramatic. Month-over-month change is only shown when the previous calendar month exists.

### Anomaly deviation (robust z-score)

- **Business meaning:** How unusual a week is compared with the previous weeks, measured in 'typical-variation units'.
- **Formula:** (Observed - Baseline) / Spread, where Baseline = median of the previous 13 complete weeks and Spread = 1.4826 x median absolute deviation of the same weeks. Flagged when |deviation| >= 4.5. Severity: moderate below 8, high to 15, extreme above
- **Data source:** Weekly revenue, profit and margin by scope (derived from sales_lines)
- **Implemented in:** `src.anomaly_detection.detect`
- **Aggregation level:** Complete Monday-Sunday weeks, per series (total, region, category, segment)
- **Intended stakeholders:** Business analyst, Business manager, Regional manager, Product/category manager
- **Interpretation caveats:** A STATISTICAL anomaly, not a confirmed business issue, and not a forecast. Uses earlier weeks only. Not scored with fewer than 8 earlier weeks or when earlier weeks are all identical. Cannot anticipate seasonal peaks. Parameters and evaluation: reports/anomaly_evaluation.md.

## Measure, calculated column or Power Query step?

Used in Phase 5 when the KPIs are rebuilt in Power BI.

| | Power Query transformation | Calculated column (DAX) | Measure (DAX) |
|---|---|---|---|
| When it runs | When data is loaded or refreshed | When data is loaded or refreshed | At query time, for the current filters |
| Result stored? | Yes, in the table | Yes, one value per row | No, computed on demand |
| Reacts to slicers? | No | No | **Yes** |
| Typical use | Clean, reshape, join, change data types | Row-level label or flag (e.g. size band) | Revenue, Profit, Margin %, AOV, period-over-period change |

All KPIs above are **measures**, because they must respond to slicers. Ratios are always measures that divide two summed measures (never an average of row-level ratios).
