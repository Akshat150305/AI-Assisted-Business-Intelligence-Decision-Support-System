# Anomaly Detection Evaluation

> **Synthetic data.** The four anomalies below were planted by `src/data_generator.py`. This report is generated by `src/anomaly_evaluation.py`; every number comes from the pipeline run.
> A flag is a **statistical anomaly** (unusual compared with the previous weeks). It is **not** a confirmed business issue and it is **not** a forecast.

## Method

- Weekly grain (Monday to Sunday), complete weeks only: 104 weeks scored.
- 39 series: revenue, profit and profit margin % for the whole business and for each region, category and customer segment.
- Baseline = median of the previous **13** complete weeks (earlier weeks only). Spread = 1.4826 x median absolute deviation of the same weeks. Deviation = (observed - baseline) / spread.
- Flagged when |deviation| >= **4.5**. Fewer than 8 valid earlier weeks: not scored. Zero spread: not scored.
- Severity: moderate below 8, high from 8, extreme from 15.

## Parameter history (full disclosure)

1. The first parameters (8-week window, threshold 3.5) were fixed **before** the detector was run on the data.
2. On the project data they flagged far too many weeks. A simulation on **pure random noise** (no project data, no labels) showed the same: they flag about 1 week in 20 even when nothing unusual happens.
3. The window and threshold were revised using **only that noise simulation**, aiming for about 1% false alarms on noise. The planted labels were not used to choose them.

| parameters | window_weeks | threshold | false_alarm_rate_pct |
|---|---|---|---|
| first choice (before any run) | 8 | 3.50 | 4.61 |
| in use | 13 | 4.50 | 0.93 |

Real weekly data flags more often than pure noise because it also has trend and seasonality, which a rolling baseline cannot anticipate.

## Limitations

- Only 4 planted events and one synthetic dataset: results do not generalise to real businesses.
- 39 series are tested every week, so some false alarms are expected by chance alone.
- A rolling baseline cannot know about seasonal peaks or planned promotions, so such weeks can be flagged.
- Slow, steady drifts are absorbed into the baseline and are not flagged.
- A flag says *where to look*. It does not say why. Causes need evidence from outside this dataset.
- Nothing here is a forecast. No predictive capability is claimed.
